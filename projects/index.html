Oni Dojo鬼道場

A complete Japanese course from kana to JLPT N1, taught by Kurenai, a mischievous oni sensei. 140 guided lessons, over 15,000 voice recordings, spaced-repetition flashcards, graded stories, speaking practice and conversations you answer out loud. Out now on Google Play: kana and N5 free, Premium for N4 to N1.

WindowsAndroidElectron · Capacitor · Supabase

How it's built

  • Offline first. Lessons, audio and progress live on the device. An account is optional, so most learners never send me any data.
  • Purchases checked on the server. The phone buys through Google Play; an edge function asks the Google Play Developer API whether each purchase is real before anything unlocks, so patched stores can't fake Premium.
  • Locked-down database. Row-level security on every table, no public write access to purchases or access levels, and accounts deleted with everything they own (GDPR).
  • Signed updates. New lessons for the PC app are signed, and the app refuses anything that doesn't match its built-in public key.
  • Voices recorded in advance. Every sentence is rendered with VOICEVOX at build time and checked against the app's own furigana, so the app never needs a speech server.
  • Automated builds. GitHub Actions validates the content, runs syntax checks and builds the signed Android bundle for Google Play.

Rireki Studio履歴書

A free tool for foreigners applying for jobs in Japan. Fill in one form in English or Japanese and get a correct 履歴書 (on the 2021 government form or the classic JIS form) and 職務経歴書 as an A4 PDF. A checker catches about 30 common mistakes, such as old dates, abbreviations like ㈱, or more than 28 hours a week on a student visa, and a guide in 11 chapters explains every box.

Everything stays in your browser: no account, no server, no tracking. I made it for my own job search, and my own CVs were its first test.

Tokoyami Oni Radio常闇鬼電波

Japanese internet radio in one player: local and community stations from all over Japan, sorted by region, with the same neon oni look as my other apps. I listen to it all day for Japanese practice. It started as a Windows app and is now rebuilt natively for Android.

  • Learn while you listen: programme titles translated or shown in romaji.
  • Never miss a show: reminders before a programme starts, an alarm clock and a sleep timer.
  • Small touches: volume per station, Chromecast, and a share card for what you're hearing.
